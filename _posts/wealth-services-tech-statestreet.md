## 403(b) - $1.6 trillion (as of June 30, 2026)

1. Traditionally, TIAA's core battlefield and absolute base has been the 403(b) market

1. 403(b) Plan: Total assets of $1.6 trillion (as of June 30, 2026).
2. TIAA is the top guy in the market. 
3. Industry surveys also consistently list it as "far ahead" of the second place (Fidelity).
4. For large institutional 403(b) plans, pure recordkeeping/administration fees are typically in the single-digit to low double-digit basis point range (0.01%–0.20%)
5. 

6. Employees contribute a portion of their salary directly through automatic payroll deductions.
7. For 2026, the standard employee contribution limit is $24,500 per year, with additional catch-up contributions allowed conditionally. 
8. Traditional 403(b): Contributions are made with pre-tax dollars, lowering your current taxable income. The money grows tax-deferred, and withdrawals in retirement are taxed as ordinary income.
9.  Roth 403(b): Contributions are made with after-tax dollars. Investments grow tax-free, and qualified withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free.


1. A 403(b) plan is a **tax-advantaged retirement savings plan**, 
2. It is often called a "tax-sheltered annuity" (TSA) plan.
3. but specifically designed for employees of 
   1. **Public Educational Organizations**: This includes public schools, colleges, and universities.
   2. **501(c)(3) Tax-Exempt Organizations**: This covers charities, private hospitals, religious institutions, and research facilities.
   3. **Churches**: For ministers and church employees, often through a "retirement income account".


1. Contributions are typically made through **salary reduction agreements** (pre-tax employee deferrals), and some employers may also make matching or nonelective contributions. 
2. The money grows tax-deferred until withdrawal, usually in retirement. 
3. If a Roth option is available, contributions are made with after-tax dollars, 
4. but qualified withdrawals are tax-free.

### 📈 Investment Options
Unlike a 401(k) which is typically invested in mutual funds, 403(b) plans have specific investment vehicles:
*   **Annuity Contracts**: Provided by insurance companies.
*   **Custodial Accounts**: Invested in mutual funds.
*   **Retirement Income Accounts**: Specifically for church employees.

## Wealth business @ TIAA 

**advice-led extension of its retirement franchise**, not a standalone private bank. 
It targets the "mass affluent" participants it already serves through workplace 403(b) plans—educators, healthcare workers, and researchers—who have accumulated savings but often lack access to traditional wealth advice. The division is currently undergoing a significant strategic and technological build-out under CEO David Nason, with a clear focus on unifying fragmented systems and scaling advice delivery.

### 🏛️ Strategic Position and Target Client

TIAA Wealth Management sits under the broader TIAA umbrella (which holds roughly **$1.5 trillion in AUM** as of late 2025). Its distinct value proposition is rooted in TIAA's century-long heritage with nonprofit institutions.

- **Target Demographic**: The core client is the "forgotten" mass affluent professional—teachers, nurses, and public servants—who have built substantial retirement assets through consistent contributions but may not believe traditional financial advice is "for them". TIAA explicitly aims to meet these clients **at and through retirement and on to the next generation**.
- **Service Model**: Rather than a single private banker model, TIAA Wealth uses a **flexible delivery structure**: digital self-service tools, team-based support, and dedicated advisors. This is designed to serve clients at different life stages and asset levels efficiently.

### 🛠️ The Technology Modernization (The "Build" Phase)

For anyone assessing the technology function, this is the most active area. TIAA is mid-flight in replacing legacy infrastructure with a unified platform to bridge its retirement recordkeeping and wealth advisory sides.

- **Core Platform Shift**: In late 2025, TIAA Wealth Management selected **BNY Pershing's Wove** platform as its unified wealth solution. This is a multi-year implementation spanning its broker-dealer, investment adviser, and bank custody businesses.
- **Key Tech Objectives**:
    - **Unification**: Wove is intended to create a "one-stop shop" so advisors and clients can see a holistic financial picture across different account types (retirement, brokerage, managed accounts) rather than navigating siloed systems.
    - **Advisor Experience (Wove Advisor)**: The platform integrates directly with **Salesforce**, aiming to give advisors an intuitive interface and automated workflows to reduce administrative friction.
    - **Client Experience (Wove Investor)**: A modern, mobile-friendly portal integrated into the TIAA website that expands self-service capabilities while allowing collaboration with advisors.
    - **AI Integration**: TIAA has explicitly stated it is deploying **AI-powered tools** alongside the Wove implementation to help advisors spend more time on personalized guidance rather than operations.

### 📦 Product and Investment Engine

The wealth products are managed and distributed through TIAA's subsidiary infrastructure (e.g., TIAA-CREF Individual & Institutional Services, TIAA Trust).

- **Managed Accounts (The Core Engine)**: TIAA's internal investment manager (WIM) supports the "TIAA Managed Accounts" programs. As of early 2026, WIM managed roughly **$47 billion discretionarily** and **$44 billion non-discretionarily** (largely as a sub-adviser to the wealth programs).
- **Product Breadth**: The wealth division offers a spectrum beyond basic retirement annuities:
    - **Managed Accounts**: Discretionary portfolio management with asset allocation models developed by WIM.
    - **Brokerage**: Self-directed trading (stocks, ETFs, mutual funds) with no account minimums or maintenance fees, often used as a "do-it-yourself" option before clients graduate to advice.
    - **Trust & Estate**: Personal trust services and estate planning tools (e.g., via Trust & Will) to capture intergenerational wealth transfer.
    - **Education Savings**: 529 plans, leveraging TIAA's role as a plan manager.

### 🎯 Implications for a Wealth Tech Leadership Role (Interview Context)

If you are evaluating a candidate for an MD-level Wealth Tech role at TIAA (or a peer), the TIAA context suggests looking for:

- **Integration & Platform Consolidation Experience**: TIAA's current pain point (and opportunity) is stitching together retirement recordkeeping data with a modern wealth custody/advisory stack. A strong candidate must have experience **consolidating disparate systems** (e.g., legacy retirement platforms with modern brokerage/advice engines) into a unified advisor and client experience.
- **Mass-Affluent Scalability**: Unlike a pure UHNW private bank, TIAA needs technology that scales advice efficiently to thousands of participants with moderate asset levels. Look for experience with **digital advice tools, hybrid advice models, and AI-driven productivity** that lower the cost-to-serve without degrading the human touch.
- **Regulatory Nuance (ERISA + Wealth)**: TIAA operates at the intersection of ERISA-governed retirement plans and standard wealth advisory (RIA/BD) regulations. A leader here must understand the compliance implications of **rollover advice** (where fiduciary duties can shift) and the data privacy boundaries between plan-level recordkeeping and individual wealth management.

In short, TIAA's Wealth business is less about exotic alternatives and more about **industrializing advice** for the American middle class. The technology mandate is to replace a patchwork of retirement and brokerage systems with a single, intelligent, and compliant platform that makes advice scalable.
