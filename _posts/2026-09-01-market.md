# The Compass Framework

## A Two-Layer Approach to Wealth Creation

### Purpose

To identify **which sectors and stocks to own** for long-term wealth creation (5, 7, or 10-year horizon), and **what action to take** based on current portfolio weights.

**Note:** Cash management is handled in a separate setup. This framework assumes the full corpus is deployed into equities.

---

## Part 1: The Two-Layer Conviction Assessment

### Layer 1: Domain (Sector) Conviction

Assess the sector on a 3-point color scale:

| Color | Meaning | Criteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **GREEN** | Highest conviction. Structural tailwinds, earnings growth >15% CAGR, fair valuation, positive institutional consensus. | Sector benefits from a structural macro driver (policy, capex cycle, demographics); earnings growth >15% over 2-3 years; valuation at or below historical average; major brokerages overweight; low geopolitical/commodity risk relative to peers. |
| **BLUE** | Neutral conviction. Steady growth, fairly valued, no major catalyst. | Earnings growth 8-12%; valuation in line with historical average; no strong re-rating catalyst; no major downside risk; offers stability and steady compounding. |
| **RED** | Lowest conviction. Structural headwinds, declining growth, overvalued. | Earnings growth <8% or declining; valuation >20% above historical average; negative institutional consensus; high exposure to macro risks; structural decline or obsolescence risk. |

### Layer 2: Company Conviction (Within the Domain)

Apply the same 3-point color scale to individual companies:

| Color | Meaning | Criteria |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **GREEN** | Highest conviction. Market leader, earnings growth >15%, strong ROE/ROCE, clear moat, analyst consensus Buy. | Best-in-class operator; market share gainer; strong return ratios; durable competitive advantage; positive analyst consensus. |
| **BLUE** | Neutral conviction. Quality business but fully priced, growth in line with market, no urgent reason to buy/sell. | Quality business but fully priced; growth in line with market; no major upside or downside catalyst currently. |
| **RED** | Lowest conviction. Losing market share, margin pressure, structural decline, analyst consensus Sell/Reduce. | Losing market share; margin pressure; fading competitive edge; analyst consensus Sell or Reduce. |

### The Marriage: What to Own for Wealth Creation

| Domain | Company | Verdict | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **GREEN** | **GREEN** | **CORE HOLDING** | Highest conviction. Wealth creation engine. |
| **GREEN** | BLUE | **SATELLITE** | Good sector, but the company is not the best. Own the leader instead. |
| **BLUE** | **GREEN** | **SELECTIVE** | The sector is average, but the company is exceptional. |
| **BLUE** | BLUE | **AVOID** | Neither the sector nor the company excites. |
| **RED** | Any | **EXIT** | Broken sector. Even the best company will struggle. |
| **Any** | RED | **EXIT** | Broken company. Do not hold, regardless of sector. |

**The Rule:** For wealth creation, **prioritize GREEN-GREEN**. Allow selective BLUE-GREEN if the company is exceptional. Avoid everything else.

## Part 2: Target Allocation

### The Allocation Math

The target invested amount for each holding is derived from the **GBR sector rating** and the **company's position within it**.

| GBR Pair | Target % of Portfolio | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **GREEN-GREEN (Core)** | **8% – 12% per stock** | Wealth creation engine. The largest allocations. |
| **GREEN-GREEN (Satellite)** | **5% – 8% per stock** | Secondary GREEN-GREEN names for diversification within GREEN sectors. |
| **BLUE-GREEN (Selective)** | **3% – 5% per stock** | Exceptional companies in average sectors. Tactical, not core. |
| **BLUE-BLUE** | **0% – 2% per stock** | Avoid unless already held for legacy reasons. |
| **RED (Any)** | **0%** | Exit completely. |
| **Reserved for Future GREEN-GREEN** | **15% – 25% of portfolio** | Dry powder for new GREEN-GREEN opportunities as they emerge. |

**Guiding Principle:** No single stock should exceed **12%** of total equity, regardless of conviction. The GREEN bucket should collectively hold **65-75%** of the portfolio.

---

## Part 3: Action Determination

Once the target allocation is set, compare **Current Weight** vs. **Target Weight** for each holding.

| Action | Condition | What It Means |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **ACCUMULATE (ACC)** | Current < Target. The gap is positive. | Buy more to reach target weight. |
| **HOLD** | Current ≈ Target (within ±2%). | No action. Maintain position. |
| **REDUCE** | Current > Target. The gap is negative. | Trim excess to return to target weight. |
| **SELL** | RED conviction. Target = 0%. | Exit completely. |
| **WATCH** | No allocation. Catalyst pending. | Track quarterly. Do not buy yet. |

### How to Read the Gap

| Gap (Target - Current) | Action | Intensity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gap > 5%** | **BUY / ACCUMULATE (Aggressive)** | Deploy capital immediately. |
| **Gap 2% – 5%** | **ACCUMULATE (Gradual)** | Buy on dips, in tranches. |
| **Gap within ±2%** | **HOLD** | Do nothing. You are at target. |
| **Gap -2% to -5%** | **REDUCE (Gradual)** | Trim on strength. |
| **Gap < -5%** | **REDUCE (Aggressive) / SELL** | Cut heavily or exit completely. |

---

## Part 4: Action Execution (The "How")

For any holding requiring **ACCUMULATE** or **REDUCE**, use the following framework to determine exact levels.

### For ACCUMULATE (Buying)

| Parameter | How to Determine |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Entry Levels** | Identify 2-3 support zones using technical analysis (moving averages, 52-week low, trendlines). Set tiered GTT buys at progressively lower prices. |
| **GTT Setup** | Trigger = entry price. Limit = 1-2% below trigger (to ensure execution). |
| **Stop-Loss** | Place below the **thesis invalidation level** (e.g., below 52-week low, below long-term trendline). Do not place at obvious round numbers where retail stops cluster. |
| **Position Sizing** | Deploy in tranches (e.g., 40%/35%/25%) to lower average cost. |

### For REDUCE (Selling)

| Parameter | How to Determine |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Exit Levels** | Identify resistance zones or use the stock's recent range high as the target. |
| **GTT Setup** | Trigger = exit price. Limit = 1-2% below trigger (to ensure execution). |
| **Book Profit / Target** | If the stock has run up significantly, book partial profits at resistance. Trail the stop-loss upward as it rallies. |
| **Full Exit** | If the thesis is broken (RED conviction), exit completely. Do not wait for a bounce. |

### For HOLD

No action required. Maintain the position. Review quarterly.

---

## Part 5: Review Cadence

| Frequency | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Quarterly (post-results)** | Re-assess GBR ratings for all holdings. Update target weights if the conviction changes. |
| **Monthly** | Check current weight vs. target weight. Identify any gaps requiring action. |
| **Event-driven** | Major catalysts (IPOs, regulatory changes, geopolitical events) trigger immediate review. |

## Summary: The Compass in One Page

| Step | Action |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1** | Assess the domain: GREEN, BLUE, or RED. |
| **2** | Assess the company within the domain: GREEN, BLUE, or RED. |
| **3** | Marry the two: prioritize GREEN-GREEN. Allow selective BLUE-GREEN. Avoid RED. |
| **4** | Assign a target weight (% of portfolio) to each holding. |
| **5** | Compare current weight vs. target weight. |
| **6** | If gap >2%: ACCUMULATE (if underweight) or REDUCE (if overweight). |
| **7** | For ACC: set tiered GTT buys, define stop-loss. For REDUCE: set GTT sells, define profit target. |
| **8** | Review quarterly. Rebalance as needed. |

## Dead cat bounce 
1. The name comes from an old Wall Street saying: "Even a dead cat will bounce if it falls from a great height."
2. prices recover briefly after a crash, but then resume their downward trend.
3. This kind of bounce looks like a "recovery," but it is actually just a temporary pause in a continuing decline, not a genuine trend reversal.
4. Check for Relative Volume (RVOL) to be approx. 2 or above. 
5. https://chartink.com/screener/copy-relative-volume-5-days-1
   

## What is whipsaw

1. a whipsaw is a market term for a situation where a stock (or any asset) moves sharply in one direction, then reverses just as sharply in the opposite direction, trapping traders who acted on the first move
2. Whipsaws are most common around major catalysts like IPOs, earnings, elections, or regulatory decisions.
